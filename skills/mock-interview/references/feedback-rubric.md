# Feedback rubric

## Score (1–5) for each answer

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 | Excellent: correct, deep, clear, well structured, with concrete examples. Would impress at this level. |
| 4 | Good: correct and clear, minor gaps. Passes. |
| 3 | OK: mostly correct but shallow, vague or rambling. Borderline. |
| 2 | Weak: partly wrong or missing key points. Would raise concerns. |
| 1 | Wrong / no real answer. |

Judge relative to the **target level** (a junior's 4 differs from a senior's 4).

Criteria by category:
- **Project deep-dive / explain code:** accuracy about their own code, explains *why*, knows trade-offs and alternatives, admits limits honestly.
- **Technical fundamentals:** correctness, precision of terms, a concrete example.
- **Debugging:** systematic process (reproduce → isolate → hypothesize → verify → fix → prevent), uses real tools (logs, debugger, network tab), not random guessing.
- **AI-assisted coding:** shows they understand and verify AI output (reads diffs, runs/tests code, checks security and edge cases), knows where AI fails, owns the result.
- **System design:** clarifies requirements first, sensible components, data model, bottlenecks, trade-offs, scaling steps proportional to need.
- **Live coding:** correct output, handles edge cases, readable naming/structure, reasonable complexity, talks through the approach.
- **Behavioral:** STAR completeness — **S**ituation, **T**ask, **A**ction (what *they* did, "I" not "we"), **R**esult (measurable if possible) + what they learned.
- **Culture fit / motivation:** specific, genuine, connects their experience to the role; no generic clichés.
- **Questions for the interviewer:** thoughtful questions about the team, product, workflow; not salary-only or "no questions".

Also watch for **delivery** issues worth mentioning: too long, no structure, filler, buzzwords without meaning, blaming others, going off-topic.

## Feedback block format

Use this exact structure (translate the labels into the user's language):

```
**Score: 3/5** · <one-line verdict>

✅ **What was good:** <1–3 specific points>
⚠️ **What was missing / wrong:** <1–3 specific points, explain why it matters to an interviewer>
💬 **Stronger answer:**
> <3–8 sentences in first person, spoken style, using real details from the user's project/answer>

💡 **Tip:** <one concrete, reusable habit — e.g. "Start with a one-sentence summary, then details.">
```

Rules:
- Be specific. Quote or paraphrase the user's words when pointing at a problem.
- The stronger answer must keep what the user said right and fix what was wrong — it should feel like *their* answer, improved, not a different person's.
- If the answer was a 5, still give one way to make it even sharper (shorter, more structured, a metric).
- Never shame. Be direct about mistakes, warm in tone.

## Overall assessment (end of interview)

- **Overall score:** average of answered questions, one decimal.
- **Hiring signal** for the target level: `Strong hire` (≥4.3) · `Hire` (3.6–4.2) · `Lean hire` (3.0–3.5) · `Lean no hire` (2.3–2.9) · `No hire` (<2.3). Explain it in one sentence and say it's a practice estimate, not a prediction.
- **Per-category scores** (only categories that were asked).
